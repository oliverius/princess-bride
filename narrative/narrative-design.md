# The Princess Bride — An Interactive Novella

**Draft:** 0.5 — dialogue-led editorial manuscript  
**Based on:** `narrative-design.v0.4.md` (retain v0.4 unchanged)  
**Format:** Fifteen story beats, with as many playable passages as each encounter needs  
**Planned runtime:** Twine / SugarCube 2  
**Status:** For editorial review; no Twine code or images yet

> **Fan-project note:** An unofficial, non-commercial fan adaptation. The narration and interactive exchanges here are newly written; they are not transcriptions of the novel or screenplay. The underlying characters and story belong to their respective rights holders.

## Reading conventions

- **Scene** means a dramatic unit tied to one of the 15 beats, **not** necessarily one Twine passage. Subscenes (`07a`, `07b`) are candidate passages, not mandatory implementation boundaries.
- Each **Choice** lists fully written consequences. **[REJOIN]** means continue from the next common paragraph; **[RETRY]** returns to that particular choice after the feedback; **[ADVANCE]** moves to the next numbered scene.
- A choice marked **[REJOIN]** need not be described as correct or incorrect. Some choices change a conversation without changing the plot.
- **Grandson/Grandfather** interruptions occur in the bedroom framing story. They should be quick and surprising, never undermine grief or genuine affection.
- Viewpoints change deliberately. The reader does not need to know the film, collect items or manage statistics.
- No illustration directions are embedded in the prose. Art will be mapped separately after editorial approval.

# ACT I — A Story Worth Interrupting

## Scene 01 — The Book on the Bedside Table
**Beat:** Opening Image  
**Viewpoint:** Grandson  
**Setting:** Bedroom, rainy afternoon

Rain tapped the window. The boy had a blanket, a glass of water and an impressive collection of toys arranged to make illness look productive.

His grandfather entered carrying a thick book.

**Grandson:** “Is that homework?”

**Grandfather:** “Worse. Literature.”

**Grandson:** “I knew you were up to something.”

**Grandfather:** “This one has sword fights, pirates, a giant and a prince who is very pleased with himself.”

**Grandson:** “Does the prince get punched?”

**Grandfather:** “You're asking excellent questions remarkably early.”

**Choice — How does the boy negotiate the reading?**

- **A. “Start with the sword fights.”** **Grandfather:** “We must first meet the people holding the swords.” **Grandson:** “Fine. But introduce them quickly.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “No kissing.”** **Grandfather:** “I can offer a reasonable amount of sword fighting in exchange.” **Grandson:** “That wasn't a yes.” **Grandfather:** “You're learning to negotiate.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “Can I choose what happens?”** **Grandfather:** “You may choose what people say and do. I reserve the right to keep the story from becoming nonsense.” **Grandson:** “That sounds unfair.” **Grandfather:** “Most authors would agree.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** The grandfather opened the book. On the first page was a farm, which the boy regarded with immediate suspicion. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 02 — The Farm and the Answer
**Beat:** Theme Stated  
**Viewpoint:** Buttercup  
**Setting:** Farm at the edge of Florin

The farm had a leaky roof, a temperamental horse and a gate that refused to close. Buttercup believed these problems could all be solved by Westley.

**Buttercup:** “The gate is broken again.”

**Westley:** “A tragedy. Shall I inform the king?”

**Buttercup:** “You could fix it.”

**Westley:** “An excellent alternative to bothering the king.”

He worked while she watched. She had recently developed the irritating habit of finding reasons to watch.

**Buttercup:** “You don't have to agree with everything I say.”

**Westley:** “I've been waiting for permission to disagree about the horse.”

**Choice — Buttercup attempts to say what she actually means.**

- **A. “I like having you here.”** **Westley:** “Even when I'm not repairing something?” **Buttercup:** “Let's not get carried away.” He smiles; she doesn't look away. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “That gate is crooked.”** Westley checks it with exaggerated care. **Westley:** “Remarkable. It was straight until you came outside.” **Buttercup:** “Are you blaming me?” **Westley:** “Only the gate would dare.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “Perhaps you should stop agreeing with me.”** **Westley:** “I disagree.” **Buttercup:** “You are impossible.” **Westley:** “We finally agree.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** Westley shut the repaired gate. Buttercup reached for the latch at the same time as he did. Neither moved their hand immediately. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 03 — The Horizon
**Beat:** Set-Up  
**Viewpoint:** Westley  
**Setting:** Farm road

Westley had decided to leave and earn enough money to return with a future worth offering. He had rehearsed a dignified farewell. The rehearsal had not included Buttercup looking at him like that.

**Buttercup:** “How long?”

**Westley:** “I don't know.”

**Buttercup:** “An ambitious answer.”

**Westley:** “I considered inventing a number. You would have noticed.”

**Choice — What does Westley leave her with?**

- **A. A practical promise.** **Westley:** “I'll write whenever I can.” **Buttercup:** “Then I'll complain about your handwriting whenever you do.” She squeezes his hand. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. A joke.** **Westley:** “Please don't let the horse manage the farm. He has expensive tastes.” **Buttercup:** “He'll miss you.” **Westley:** “I was hoping someone would.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. A grand declaration.** **Westley:** “Nothing in the world will keep me away forever.” **Buttercup:** “You haven't seen much of the world.” **Westley:** “Then it had better be on its best behaviour.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** He walked down the road. Buttercup watched until she could no longer see him. Neither had said the most important thing plainly, but both had heard it. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 04 — A Letter Without Hope
**Beat:** Catalyst  
**Viewpoint:** Buttercup  
**Setting:** Farm, months later

A messenger arrived with news of a ship attacked by pirates. Westley had been aboard. No survivors were known.

**Messenger:** “I'm sorry. I wish I had better news.”

Buttercup looked at the road beyond him.

**Buttercup:** “Do you know for certain?”

**Messenger:** “No.”

**Buttercup:** “Then don't ask me to be certain for you.”

**Choice — What does she do next?**

- **A. Ask for every detail.** The messenger tells her what little he knows. She listens carefully, as if careful listening could bring him home. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Close the door.** She shuts out the messenger, but not his words. After a long while she opens the door again. He has gone. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Return to the day's work.** She carries a bucket to the gate, sets it down, and cannot remember why she picked it up. **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** Westley's gate still closed perfectly. Buttercup hated it for that, then hated herself for hating it. Seasons passed. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 05 — The Prince's Proposal
**Beat:** Debate  
**Viewpoint:** Buttercup  
**Setting:** Palace gardens

Prince Humperdinck had a talent for turning a conversation into an announcement.

**Humperdinck:** “Our marriage will delight the kingdom.”

**Buttercup:** “Has the kingdom been consulted?”

**Humperdinck:** “It will be delighted. I have arranged it.”

He gestured towards a row of gardeners, all suddenly working with unnatural enthusiasm.

**Humperdinck:** “You will have jewels, gowns, guards and whatever else a princess requires.”

**Buttercup:** “A choice?”

**Humperdinck:** “Within reason.”

**Buttercup:** “And who decides what's reasonable?”

**Humperdinck:** “I admire your interest in government.”

**Choice — Buttercup tests the prince's sincerity.**

- **A. “What if I don't love you?”** **Humperdinck:** “People adore the royal family without knowing us personally.” **Buttercup:** “That explains a great deal.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “What exactly would you expect?”** **Humperdinck:** “Attend ceremonies. Smile. Avoid surprises.” **Buttercup:** “I can promise to attend.” **Humperdinck:** “Splendid.” **Buttercup:** “I said nothing about the other two.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “I need time to think.”** **Humperdinck:** “Take the afternoon.” **Buttercup:** “Generous.” **Humperdinck:** “I'm known for it.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** Buttercup accepted a future she did not want, convinced that the one she did want had disappeared at sea. The palace began ordering flowers by the cartload. **[ADVANCE]**

# ACT II — The Adventure Refuses to Behave

## Scene 06 — Three Unlikely Kidnappers
**Beat:** Break into Two  
**Viewpoint:** Buttercup  
**Setting:** Lonely road, then a boat

The kidnapping was efficient only if you ignored the shouting. A small man issued commands, a giant lifted Buttercup with surprising gentleness, and a swordsman apologised twice before they reached the boat.

**Vizzini:** “A flawless operation!”

**Buttercup:** “We're still within sight of the shore.”

**Vizzini:** “A flawless operation in progress.”

**Fezzik:** “Should we offer her a blanket?”

**Vizzini:** “We're kidnappers!”

**Fezzik:** “It's windy.”

**Choice — Whom does Buttercup question?**

- **A. Fezzik: “Are you always this polite?”** **Fezzik:** “Not when I'm asleep.” **Buttercup:** “Fair enough.” He offers the blanket anyway. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Iñigo: “You don't seem proud of this work.”** **Iñigo:** “I have performed better work. Some of it involved less shouting.” **Vizzini, from the stern:** “I HEARD THAT!” **Iñigo:** “That is what I mean.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Vizzini: “What happens if your plan fails?”** **Vizzini:** “Then I shall demonstrate why failure was the cleverest possible outcome.” **Buttercup:** “How convenient.” **[REJOIN]**

A black sail appeared behind them.

**Fezzik:** “Someone is following us.”

**Vizzini:** “Impossible. I planned not to be followed.”

**Buttercup:** “Perhaps they didn't receive the plan.”

**Continuation:** Vizzini ordered the boat towards the Cliffs of Insanity. His crew exchanged the sort of look that suggested they had already regretted several career decisions. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 07 — The Stranger in Black
**Beat:** B Story  
**Viewpoint:** Iñigo  
**Setting:** Cliffs of Insanity

### 07a — A Courteous Ambush

The stranger in black climbed the last stretch of rock. Iñigo waited at the top with his sword sheathed.

**Stranger:** “Are you going to attack me?”

**Iñigo:** “Eventually. It seems rude to interrupt a man who has just climbed a cliff.”

**Stranger:** “You have an unusual sense of hospitality.”

**Iñigo:** “I was raised properly. I also intend to win.”

**Stranger:** “A host with modest ambitions.”

**Choice — How does Iñigo open the conversation?**

- **A. “Who taught you the sword?”** **Stranger:** “Several men. Some were better teachers after I stopped listening.” **Iñigo:** “A familiar educational method.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “Would you like to surrender?”** **Stranger:** “Would you?” **Iñigo:** “No.” **Stranger:** “Then we have saved ourselves two speeches.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “I have trained for twenty years.”** **Stranger:** “Excellent. I had hoped not to spend the afternoon instructing a beginner.” **Iñigo:** “I am beginning to dislike you.” **Stranger:** “That usually happens after the duel.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** They drew their swords. **[ADVANCE WITHIN SCENE → 07b]**

### 07b — The Polite Duel

Steel rang against steel. Iñigo stepped forward; the stranger gave ground, then stole it back. Their blades moved faster than their conversation, which annoyed them both.

**Iñigo:** “You are very good.”

**Stranger:** “Thank you. You are making it difficult to be smug.”

**Iñigo:** “I can slow down if you need time.”

**Stranger:** “Please don't. I'd hate to become overconfident.”

**Choice — Iñigo tries a new tactic.**

- **A. Attempt a flamboyant flourish.** The stranger watches the elaborate movement. **Stranger:** “Beautiful. Does it come with music?” **Iñigo:** “Only if you survive the next part.” They exchange a rapid series of blows. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Fight in absolute silence.** After several tense exchanges, the stranger says, **“Are we sulking now? I wasn't informed.”** Iñigo almost laughs, which costs him a step. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Offer a critique of the stranger's stance.** **Iñigo:** “Your left foot is too far back.” The stranger adjusts and immediately gains ground. **Stranger:** “Much better. I appreciate the lesson.” **Iñigo:** “I regret the lesson.” **[REJOIN]**

The stranger's blade found Iñigo's guard and knocked the sword from his hand. Iñigo stood unarmed at the cliff edge.

**Iñigo:** “You could finish this.”

**Stranger:** “I could. But then who would tell people how impressive I was?”

He stepped away, leaving Iñigo alive.

**Iñigo:** “I would have described you as insufferable.”

**Stranger:** “I'll settle for accurate.”

**Grandson:** “Wait, he lets him live? After all that fighting?”

**Grandfather:** “Some men can win a duel without insisting on winning an argument with a corpse.”

**Grandson:** “That was a strange sentence.”

**Grandfather:** “It's a strange story.”

**Continuation:** The stranger hurried after the kidnappers. Iñigo recovered his sword, his pride, and a useful new respect for men in black. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 08 — A Giant, a Genius, and Two Cups
**Beat:** Fun and Games  
**Viewpoint:** Westley  
**Setting:** Cliff path and clearing

### 08a — The Gentle Obstacle

Fezzik stood across the path, broad enough to block it without trying.

**Fezzik:** “I have to stop you.”

**Westley:** “You sound disappointed.”

**Fezzik:** “I don't like hurting people.”

**Westley:** “Neither do I. This is already the nicest ambush I've had.”

**Choice — How does Westley handle the giant?**

- **A. “Perhaps we could discuss a compromise.”** **Fezzik:** “Vizzini says compromises are for people who lose.” **Westley:** “Does he say that often?” **Fezzik:** “Usually when he's losing.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “You are enormous. Is that an advantage?”** **Fezzik:** “Sometimes I bump my head.” **Westley:** “An unexpected weakness. I'll make a note.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “I surrender.”** **Fezzik:** “Oh. That's a relief.” He moves aside. **Westley:** “I meant I surrender the opportunity to be crushed. I'm still going through.” **Fezzik:** “That's a very confusing surrender.” **[REJOIN]**

They grappled. Fezzik was stronger, Westley faster. At last the giant fell unconscious, more gently than a man of his size ought to have been able to fall.

**Westley:** “I hope he wakes with a good opinion of me.”

**Continuation:** Ahead, Vizzini waited with Buttercup, a small table and two cups. **[ADVANCE WITHIN SCENE → 08b]**

### 08b — The Battle of Wits

**Vizzini:** “At last! A contest worthy of my intellect.”

**Westley:** “I was expecting a rescue. I seem to have arrived at a lecture.”

**Vizzini:** “One of these cups is dangerous. You must reason your way to the safe one.”

**Buttercup:** “Does he know which is which?”

**Westley:** “I was about to ask.”

**Vizzini:** “Of course I know. I have deduced it.”

**Westley:** “From what?”

**Vizzini:** “From the fact that I am clever.”

**Buttercup:** “A surprisingly economical investigation.”

**Choice 1 — How does Westley challenge Vizzini's reasoning?**

- **A. “Explain your deduction from the beginning.”** Vizzini begins with his childhood, moves on to the history of cups, and arrives at a lengthy complaint about teachers. **Westley:** “We've established you're upset with education. Can we return to the drinks?” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “What if I switch the cups?”** Vizzini snatches both cups away. **Vizzini:** “You cannot outwit me with a trick I have already anticipated!” **Westley:** “Then why did you move them?” **Vizzini:** “To demonstrate my anticipation.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “I refuse to participate in a contest without written rules.”** Vizzini demands paper. There is none. He attempts to write rules on the table with a twig. **Buttercup:** “You appear to be losing to stationery.” **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** Vizzini put the cups down with exaggerated care.

**Vizzini:** “Now choose.”

**Westley:** “You first.”

**Vizzini:** “I cannot. That would reveal my strategy.”

**Westley:** “You have a strategy?”

**Vizzini:** “Several. They contradict one another to confuse the enemy.”

### 08c — A Terrible Final Argument

**Choice 2 — What does Westley say next?**

- **A. “Then choose the cup you believe I want.”** **Vizzini:** “A simple reverse-psychology trap. Unless you know that I know, in which case—” He stops, offended by his own sentence. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “Your safest option is to refuse both.”** **Vizzini:** “I refuse to be told what I refuse!” He takes a cup merely to prove his independence. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “Let's ask Buttercup to judge.”** **Buttercup:** “My judgement is that neither of you should be trusted with refreshments.” **Vizzini:** “A biased tribunal!” **[REJOIN]**

Vizzini made his decision with a triumphant smile. Moments later, the smile deserted him. His cleverness had not protected him from the trap.

**Grandson:** “Was that really a battle of wits?”

**Grandfather:** “It was certainly a battle. Only one participant brought any wits.”

**Grandson:** “Buttercup?”

**Grandfather:** “An excellent answer.”

**Continuation:** Buttercup was free, though she had no reason yet to trust the masked man who had rescued her. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 09 — The Face Beneath the Mask
**Beat:** Midpoint  
**Viewpoint:** Buttercup  
**Setting:** Open hills

The masked stranger led Buttercup away from the clearing. He spoke carelessly of Westley, as though a man lost at sea could be dismissed with a shrug.

**Buttercup:** “You know nothing about him.”

**Stranger:** “Perhaps less than you think.”

**Buttercup:** “Then stop speaking as if you do.”

They stumbled on the slope. His mask came loose.

Westley.

Buttercup stared. He looked older and tired, but it was him.

**Choice — What are her first words?**

- **A. “I thought you were dead.”** **Westley:** “I know.” **Buttercup:** “That was a dreadful thing to let me believe.” **Westley:** “Yes. I'm sorry.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “You let me argue with you!”** **Westley:** “You were winning.” She tried to stay angry, then reached for him. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “Please tell me I'm not imagining this.”** He took her hand. **Westley:** “You're not.” For once, he had nothing clever to add. **[REJOIN]**

**Continuation:** They held each other. The reunion was real. So were the distant riders gathering on the hills behind them. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 10 — The Fire Swamp
**Beat:** Bad Guys Close In  
**Viewpoint:** Westley  
**Setting:** Fire Swamp

The swamp had a poor reputation, which Westley began to think was generous. The ground steamed. Something large moved under the reeds.

**Buttercup:** “Is there a safer route?”

**Westley:** “Almost certainly.”

**Buttercup:** “Why aren't we taking it?”

**Westley:** “I haven't found it.”

Flames burst from the earth a few steps ahead.

**Buttercup:** “That seems like useful information.”

**Westley:** “We should put up a sign.”

**Choice — How do they proceed?**

- **A. Follow the firm stones.** They pick their way slowly between vents. **Buttercup:** “You could have suggested this earlier.” **Westley:** “I was gathering evidence against the other routes.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Cut through the reeds.** Something huge splashes towards them. They retreat at speed. **Buttercup:** “Evidence gathered?” **Westley:** “Convincingly.” **[RETRY]**
- **C. Climb a fallen tree to scout.** Westley spots a narrow passage. **Buttercup:** “And the riders?” **Westley:** “Also spotted. They are inconsiderately persistent.” **[REJOIN]**

**Grandson (after B):** “Why would anyone go into the reeds after hearing a monster?”

**Grandfather:** “Some people insist on testing a warning personally.”

**Grandson:** “That's what the sign was for!”

**Grandfather:** “An excellent point. Shall I send the author a letter?”

**Continuation:** They emerged scratched and exhausted, directly in front of Humperdinck and his armed men.

**Humperdinck:** “You have led me on an unpleasant chase.”

**Buttercup:** “We were hoping you'd stop.”

Westley was seized. Buttercup was taken back to the palace. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 11 — The Machine
**Beat:** All Is Lost  
**Viewpoint:** Iñigo  
**Setting:** Castle outskirts

Within the castle, Count Rugen oversaw a machine built to cause suffering. Westley was its prisoner. By the time Fezzik found news of him, the man in black was barely alive.

Iñigo had searched for Rugen for years. The knowledge that the count was close made his hands shake, but it did not make his next decision easier.

**Fezzik:** “We need to help our friend.”

**Iñigo:** “He defeated me. I have been trying to decide whether that makes us friends.”

**Fezzik:** “He didn't kill you.”

**Iñigo:** “A persuasive argument.”

**Choice — Iñigo proposes a rescue plan.**

- **A. “We storm the gates.”** **Fezzik:** “I could throw you over the wall.” **Iñigo:** “And then?” **Fezzik:** “I haven't planned the landing.” **Iñigo:** “We shall keep thinking.” **[RETRY]**
- **B. “We find someone who can save a dying man.”** **Fezzik:** “I know a healer who argues with patients about whether they're alive.” **Iñigo:** “A professional optimist?” **Fezzik:** “More of a professional arguer.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “We disguise ourselves as guards.”** Fezzik holds a guard's jacket against his chest. It covers one shoulder. **Fezzik:** “Maybe if I don't breathe.” **Iñigo:** “I'd prefer you did.” **[RETRY]**

**Continuation:** They carried Westley towards a cottage outside town. Iñigo's revenge would have to wait. Saving a friend could not. **[ADVANCE]**

# ACT III — An Ending Worth Earning

## Scene 12 — A Very Unpromising Miracle
**Beat:** Dark Night of the Soul  
**Viewpoint:** Fezzik  
**Setting:** Miracle Max's cottage

### 12a — The Consultation

Miracle Max opened the door, saw Westley, and tried to close it again.

**Max:** “Retired.”

**Valerie (inside):** “You said that yesterday.”

**Max:** “Yesterday I was younger.”

**Fezzik:** “Our friend needs help.”

**Max:** “Most people do. It's why I retired.”

Valerie pushed past him and looked at Westley.

**Valerie:** “Bring him in.”

**Max:** “We have not discussed the fee!”

**Valerie:** “We have not discussed whether you're going to be useful.”

**Choice — Fezzik tries to persuade Max.**

- **A. Appeal to compassion.** **Fezzik:** “He's our friend.” **Max:** “A terrible argument. Friends are notoriously difficult to invoice.” Valerie brings a blanket. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Mention Humperdinck.** **Fezzik:** “The prince did this.” **Max:** “That peacock? Why didn't you begin with the diagnosis?” **Valerie:** “Because that's not a diagnosis.” **Max:** “It is in this house.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Offer everything in Fezzik's pockets.** He produces a button, a stone and some string. Max inspects the stone. **Max:** “Excellent stone. The rest is offensive.” **Fezzik:** “I can take the string back.” **Max:** “Don't be hasty.” **[REJOIN]**

### 12b — The Miracle

Max examined Westley, frowned, examined him again, and announced three incompatible conclusions.

**Iñigo:** “Can you save him?”

**Max:** “That depends on whether he intends to cooperate.”

**Valerie:** “He's unconscious.”

**Max:** “An unpromising start.”

**Choice — What should Fezzik do while Max works?**

- **A. Ask for a medical explanation.** Max gives one so long and contradictory that Valerie interrupts to translate: **“He's trying.”** **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Hold Westley's hand.** Fezzik does, carefully. **Max:** “Good. If he wakes up frightened, tell him I'm the competent one.” **Valerie:** “Don't lie to the patient.” **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Suggest a second opinion.** **Max:** “Valerie!” **Valerie:** “I agree with the giant.” **Max:** “I withdraw my request.” **[REJOIN]**

At last Westley opened his eyes. His body was far less enthusiastic about the development.

**Westley:** “Did it work?”

**Max:** “You are speaking.”

**Westley:** “Is that a yes?”

**Valerie:** “It's the closest you're going to get.”

**Grandson:** “Is Max a real doctor?”

**Grandfather:** “He has an excellent bedside manner, provided the bed is somewhere else.”

**Grandson:** “So no?”

**Grandfather:** “So let's be grateful Valerie was there.”

**Continuation:** Westley could speak and think, but barely move. That was enough for his friends to begin planning something spectacularly foolish. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 13 — Three Against a Castle
**Beat:** Break into Three  
**Viewpoint:** Iñigo  
**Setting:** Hill overlooking the castle

The castle had high walls, armed guards and a wedding underway. The rescue party consisted of one giant, one swordsman and one man who could barely sit upright.

**Iñigo:** “We need an army.”

**Westley:** “We have Fezzik.”

**Fezzik:** “I am not an army.”

**Westley:** “Try not to disappoint us.”

**Choice — How do they frighten the guards?**

- **A. Create a monstrous silhouette.** Fezzik raises a cloaked figure above the gate while Westley supplies an ominous warning. The guards scatter. **Fezzik:** “I think I frightened them.” **Iñigo:** “You frightened me, and I knew it was you.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Challenge the captain to a duel.** **Captain:** “Archers!” **Iñigo:** “I was hoping for a more intimate contest.” Arrows strike the ground nearby. **[RETRY]**
- **C. Pretend to be two enormous armies.** Fezzik shouts both sides of an imaginary argument. **Guard:** “Why do they all sound like one man?” **Westley:** “An excellent question. Retreat.” **[RETRY]**

**Grandson:** “They're attacking a castle with three people?”

**Grandfather:** “Technically two and a half. One can barely stand.”

**Grandson:** “That's a terrible plan.”

**Grandfather:** “Yes. Fortunately, it's also a very loud one.”

**Continuation:** The guards fled the gate. Iñigo and Fezzik carried Westley inside before anyone could reconsider. **[ADVANCE]**

## Scene 14 — The Castle and the Choice
**Beat:** Finale  
**Viewpoints:** Iñigo, then Buttercup  
**Setting:** Castle corridors and chamber

### 14a — The Man Iñigo Sought

Iñigo found Count Rugen in a corridor lined with tapestries. The count recognised him too late to pretend otherwise.

**Rugen:** “You are making a serious mistake.”

**Iñigo:** “I've had years to consider it.”

**Rugen:** “I can pay you.”

**Iñigo:** “You have misunderstood the debt.”

**Choice — How does Iñigo answer Rugen's attempt to escape responsibility?**

- **A. “You cannot bargain away what you did.”** Rugen's smile falters. He reaches for his weapon. **[REJOIN]**
- **B. “For once, stop talking.”** Rugen takes a breath to object. Iñigo draws his sword. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. “You should have remembered my father.”** **Rugen:** “I remember many men.” **Iñigo:** “Then remember this one.” **[REJOIN]**

Their duel was brutal. Rugen fought to survive; Iñigo fought for the father he had lost. At last Rugen fell.

Iñigo stood still. He had imagined this moment for years. It did not return a single lost day.

**Iñigo:** “I thought it would feel different.”

No one answered. For a moment, the castle was quiet.

**Continuation:** Elsewhere, Buttercup faced a different sort of enemy. **[ADVANCE WITHIN SCENE → 14b]**

### 14b — The Prince's Last Performance

Humperdinck entered Buttercup's chamber with the satisfied air of a man expecting applause.

**Humperdinck:** “Everything is under control.”

**Buttercup:** “You keep saying that. Is it a royal motto?”

The door opened again. Westley stood in the doorway, pale and unsteady, supporting himself against the frame.

**Humperdinck:** “You!”

**Westley:** “I'm told that is my name.”

**Humperdinck:** “Guards!”

No one came.

**Buttercup:** “Perhaps they've found better employment.”

**Choice — How does Buttercup seize the moment?**

- **A. Stand beside Westley.** She crosses the room. **Humperdinck:** “You cannot simply ignore my orders!” **Buttercup:** “I've been practising.” **[REJOIN]**
- **B. Demand that the prince explain himself.** Humperdinck launches into a speech about duty and the realm. **Buttercup:** “Can you give us the version without yourself in it?” He cannot. **[REJOIN]**
- **C. Pretend to obey.** She steps towards the prince and slides his sword beyond his reach. **Westley:** “A convincing performance.” **Buttercup:** “I learned from an expert.” **[REJOIN]**

**Humperdinck:** “You cannot defeat me!”

**Westley:** “Then it must be terribly embarrassing that you already look defeated.”

Humperdinck looked at Buttercup, the empty doorway and the sword he could no longer reach. He chose not to test his courage.

### 14c — The Escape

Iñigo and Fezzik arrived in time to find Westley leaning against the wall.

**Fezzik:** “Can you walk?”

**Westley:** “In theory.”

**Iñigo:** “I suggest we test that theory outside the castle.”

# Editorial and implementation notes

1. **Fifteen beats, not fifteen passages.** The 15 numbered scenes are structural units. Scenes 07, 08, 12 and 14 have explicit subscenes; Codex may split or combine passages further as needed for readable pacing.
2. **Choice model.** Most options give distinctive feedback and reconverge. Only choices that genuinely block progress use `[RETRY]`; there is no punitive game-over state.
3. **Comic rhythm.** Jokes are driven by character: Westley's dry confidence, Buttercup's incisive questions, Iñigo's courtly intensity, Fezzik's literal kindness, Vizzini's circular reasoning, Humperdinck's vanity, and Max and Valerie's domestic sparring.
4. **Emotional contrast.** Scene 04, the reunion in 09, and Iñigo's confrontation in 14 are deliberately less jokey. Preserve that contrast in future edits.
5. **Framing interruptions.** The grandson and grandfather interrupt at specific beats rather than after every scene. Their dialogue may become its own Twine passage or a short overlay.
6. **No inventory, statistics or alternate endings.** These would add scope without improving this version's purpose.
7. **Art later.** Once the text is approved, prepare a scene-to-illustration list using `reference/style-guide.md` and the approved character reference sheets.
8. **Copyright/editorial guardrail.** Keep new dialogue original. Do not paste in signature speeches or recreate lengthy screenplay exchanges.
